The showcased "Aa" demonstrates the clean, modern aesthetic of the brand face,
highlighting its clarity and versatility across applications.

One family across everything is a deliberate constraint — it forces hierarchy to come from
size, weight and space rather than from a second typeface.

<Block title="Typography" subtitle="Weights">
<Fragment slot="note">

Two weights do most of the work. **Bold** for headlines and anything that must be seen
first; **Light** for running text, where it reads as calm rather than thin. The weights in
between exist for interface work — labels, buttons, table headers.

</Fragment>

<Block title="Typography" subtitle="Typescale">
<Fragment slot="note">

The scale runs from large headline sizes down to small body text, keeping readability and
visual harmony. Headlines use the bold weight to command attention; body text uses the
light weight for easy reading.

Every step has a job. If a design needs something between two steps, it needs one of the
two steps.

<Callout tone="note" title="On the web">

Sizes use `clamp()`, so each step scales with the viewport between a floor and a ceiling.
The print values remain the reference for anything laid out in InDesign.

</Callout>

</Fragment>

<TypeScale />

</Block>

<Block title="Typography" subtitle="Setting rules">
<Fragment slot="note">

Left-aligned, ragged right. No justification, no centred paragraphs longer than two lines.

Measure between 45 and 75 characters. Headlines get tighter tracking as they get larger;
body copy is never tracked.

Sentence case everywhere. Uppercase is reserved for small labels, where it is tracked out
to stay legible.

</Fragment>
