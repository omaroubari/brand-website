## Light style

</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="note">

Airy, high-key, generous with negative space. Used for the studio, the team and work in
progress — anything that should feel open and unguarded.

Type sits directly on these images in black, so shoot with a quiet area reserved for it.

</Fragment>

## Dark style

</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="note">

Low-key, high-contrast, weighted towards depth. Used for campaign and product imagery
where the message needs gravity.

Type reverses to white; keep the subject away from the corner the type will occupy.

</Fragment>

## Duotone

</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="note">

Where an image needs to belong to the brand more than it needs to be literal, it is
reduced to greyscale and laid over the accent.

Use sparingly — covers, dividers, campaign headers — and never for a photograph of a
person's face.

</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="copy">

## Selection

</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="note">

Real over staged. If a stock image looks like stock, it is the wrong image.

Composition should be simple enough to survive a crop to square and to 16:9. Avoid
photographs with their own strong colour story — they compete with the accent rather than
sitting beneath it.

<Callout tone="note" title="Licensing">

Every image needs a licence on file before it ships, including social. Record the source
and the licence term alongside the asset.

</Callout>
