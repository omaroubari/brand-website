## Primary colours

Black and white are the foundation. They carry the logotype, typography, surfaces and
the high-contrast moments of the identity.

<ColorPalette category="primary" />

## Secondary colours

Orange/Red creates focus. The neutral scale provides the quieter structure around it;
use the signal colour deliberately, and let the primary colours do most of the work.

<ColorPalette category="secondary" />

## Palette

These are the approved digital shades for interface work. `neutral` and `orange` are
stable token families; the named colours above are their reference points. Use a numbered
shade only where a UI needs a measured step lighter or darker.

<ColorScale />

## Proportion

Black and white carry the work. The neutral scale supplies rules, secondary surfaces and
quiet type. Orange/Red is the exception, not the rule: roughly one part signal colour to
nine parts everything else.

If a layout needs more accent to feel finished, the layout is the problem.

## Contrast

Every pairing, measured. Anything under 4.5:1 fails for body copy at normal sizes; the
struck-through values are not available to you.

<Callout tone="note">

Large text — 24 px and up, or 19 px bold — only needs 3:1. Icons and input borders also
need 3:1 against what is behind them.

</Callout>
